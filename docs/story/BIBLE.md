# OVERDUB — story bible

Two pages. The cast is in `CAST.md`, every shipped line is in `data/story/story.json`, and the
engineering notes are in `INTEGRATION.md`. Nothing here overrides `docs/requirements.txt`; where the
requirements leave something open (chapters 18 and 22), this document makes the smallest choice
that gives the game an ending, and says so.

## Throughline

Moxie goes down for the score nobody has pulled: whatever is at the bottom of the ruins, which
rumour says is a time machine and which everyone agrees takes a crew of four to reach. Moxie does
not do crews. She has a Walkman with a strange recording head in it, and on the first floor she
finds out it lets her be her own crew. Twelve floors later she has spent a whole descent relying on
takes of herself she would once have thrown away, and when the machine offers her the chance to
record over any moment of her life, she does the other thing a tape deck can do. She leaves every
take where it is and plays the next one on top. She walks out with a mixtape.

## What Moxie wants, and how it changes

**At the top:** money and bragging rights, in that order when asked and the other order in fact.
She scavenges because she likes it, and she works alone because splitting a take (the loot kind)
has always felt like admitting she needed someone. She is not sad about this. She is smug about it.

**On the way down:** the game's mechanic is her flaw turned into a tool. She would rather do a
job four times herself than ask once, and the ruins let her. But four takes only work if each one
leaves room for the others, holds a door it never walks through, or dies in the right place so the
next one lives. She starts by bossing her takes around ("Take Two never listens"), then bickers
with them, then is quietly grateful to them. She never says "I learned to rely on people". She
says "best crew I ever had", and it is funny because it is four of her, and true anyway.

**At the bottom:** she stops wanting to sell it. What she takes home is the thing she made, and
one thing that belongs to somebody else.

## The ruins, in three facts

1. They are older than anyone's records.
2. They were built to **keep** things. The rune-inscribed metal holds time the way tape holds
   sound. A room made of it can be wound back to its first beat.
3. People came later, more than once, and bolted their own machines onto it. Nobody finished.

The dialogue never states even this much outright; these are the facts the lines are written
against. Everything else is texture: graffiti, cables, a dead console.

## How the fiction carries the mechanics

- **The Walkman.** An ordinary belt cassette player with one extraordinary part: a four-track
  recording head that came out of the ruins thirty years ago and sat in a drawer in Opal's shop
  until Moxie bought the deck "as-is". The room is the tape; the Walkman is only the head. Outside
  the ruins it plays cassettes and nothing else.
- **Recording herself.** The head lets one person lay four takes into a room. When a take ends the
  room winds back to the count-in and plays what it kept, with her in it again. Four tracks is a
  hardware limit, which is why it is always four.
- **Dying on tape.** A take that ends badly is still a take. The metal keeps performances, not
  injuries: one hit and that Moxie is cut from the mix. The tape is still rolling, so she can
  finish the take as a ghost, but a ghost's work does not stay on the record. Then the room
  rewinds and she is at the door with the lesson. Moxie finds this alarming for one floor and
  convenient ever after.
- **Desync.** A recorded take does not know the room has changed. If a later take shuts the door
  it once walked through, the picture carries on and the girl gets left behind; she has to run
  round and catch herself up. Moxie calls it "skipping", like a scratched record.
- **Retry and reset.** A take she discards was never written. Wiping the whole tape is her
  firing and rehiring the entire crew.
- **Why the Archivist remembers.** Every room reports to something. The Archivist is the part of
  the ruins that receives what the rooms keep, so it is not inside any room and nothing rewinds
  it. A committed take is filed with it and it has read the file. A discarded take never reached
  it, so it cannot know she discarded anything. That is the whole of its advantage, and it tells
  her so.

## The Archivist's side

It is not guarding treasure. The machine at the bottom records **over**: wind the reel back, play
one new take, and everything after that beat is gone, including everything the Archivist has ever
kept. It has watched people come down wanting exactly that. It is courteous, exact and not cruel,
and it would like her to turn round. It quotes her own earlier lines back to her, because it kept
those too.

## The ending

Level 11 does not kill the Archivist. Moxie beats it by committing four takes it has no file for,
and it steps aside: all four are kept, and it tells her to go and look, then decide what it loses.
Moxie does not work out its motive aloud; that one line is all the explanation the game gives.

Level 12 is the master deck, a tape machine the size of a room, one reel holding every beat since
the place was built, hers near the end. It is a literal time machine and it would work. She runs
through what she would fix (a haircut, a red bike, "that one night"; the game never says which
night) and then notices the room still wants four of her, and solves it.

Hearing the four takes play back together is the turn. Every take she ever wanted to cut was
holding a door open for the next one. She says that once and does not draw the moral. So she does
not rewind. She asks the deck to **record**: all four of her, mixed down to
one cassette. The Archivist, after a pause, says only that nobody has asked it to keep anything in
a long time. (That keeping is what the deck was built for is the reading we intend; it is never
said.) Moxie adds that Opal's three takes are coming up with her, and it does not object. It
files her: "Moxie. Master. Kept."

On the surface the radio comes back. Opal asks whether she found it. It is bolted to the planet
and Moxie would not sell it anyway. She came up with a mixtape, and with a
second cassette that had Opal's name on it: three tracks, laid down thirty years ago by someone
who turned back before the fourth. Moxie played the fourth. Opal offers forty for the mixtape. It
is not for sale. Put the kettle on.

The shape: four takes of one person made something together, and the person who only ever worked
with herself used the last of it to finish someone else's song.

## Left unexplained on purpose

- Who built the ruins, what they were keeping, and who the later builders were.
- What the runes say.
- What the Archivist is, whether it was made or grew, and whether it is the only one.
- Which night Moxie would have gone back for.
- Why Opal turned back at three takes. She says "somebody had sense" and that is all she says.
- Whether anyone has ever used the master deck. (Nobody could tell.)
- Whether the Walkman's head still works in other ruins. Sequel-shaped, not answered.
- Moxie's surname, age and where she grew up.

## Tone rules for anyone adding lines

Dry, warm, a little cocky. Jokes come from who is talking, never from a reference or a pun for its
own sake. Moxie undersells danger and oversells herself. Nobody explains a mechanic; the briefing
does that. Nobody explains the ruins or the deck either: the Archivist says "records over" once
and the rest is implied. The Archivist only knows committed takes. Moxie never states the moral.
One idea per line, under 90 characters, and when in doubt cut the line.
